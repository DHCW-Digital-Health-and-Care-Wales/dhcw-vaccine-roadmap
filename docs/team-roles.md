# Team roles

DHCW uses the Government Digital and Data Profession Capability Framework (GDaD, formerly known as DDaT) for all professional roles in product and service delivery. It is the basis on which we describe roles, evaluate jobs, and plan career paths.

This page describes how those roles come together to form a DHCW product team. A team is not a list of names. It is a working group with a shared purpose, a shared set of practices, and a shared responsibility for what it delivers.

## Core and attached roles

A DHCW product team has two kinds of member.

Core roles are present at every phase of the service lifecycle and are the team's centre of gravity. They own the ways of working, the backlog, and the code.

Attached roles join the team for a period to do specific work. That period may be a discovery, an alpha, a major piece of design, or a technical decision that shapes future direction. Attached roles are not visitors. When you attach to a team, you are on that team. If the sprint is two weeks, you are on that team for two weeks: you attend the ceremonies, you contribute to refinement, you take collective responsibility for the sprint goal.

The distinction matters because it makes the composition of a team honest. It also makes clear that specialist capability is finite and is prioritised across the portfolio, not permanently allocated.

## The core

Two roles are always present at every phase, from discovery through to live running:

- Product Owner. Maximises the value of the product. Owns the backlog, defines priorities, and represents user and business needs to the team. The Product Owner is the decision-maker on what gets built and in what order. In a self-organising team the Product Owner holds the problem space: what we are building, for whom, and why.
- Delivery Manager. Optimises team delivery, removes blockers, coaches the team in agile practices, and facilitates ceremonies. Protects the team from external interruptions and creates the conditions for a healthy pace. The Delivery Manager holds the team space: how we are working together, what is getting in the way, and what needs to change. The Delivery Manager serves the team, not the other way around.

Neither role directs the team from above. They have a joint, collaborative relationship at the heart of the team, and their responsibilities are complementary.

The rest of the core is the delivery capability itself. It grows and contracts with the size of the service, but its members are permanent to the team:

| GDaD role | DHCW band | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software Engineer | Band 4 to 6 | Designs, builds, and maintains production-grade code. Writes tests, reviews colleagues' code, and takes shared responsibility for the quality of what the team ships. Engineers at DHCW contribute across the full delivery lifecycle. |
| Senior Software Engineer | Band 7 | Owns the technical vision and architectural integrity of the product day-to-day. Mentors the engineering team, evaluates feasibility, and works closely with the Product Owner to ensure technical investment is prioritised alongside feature delivery. |
| Test Engineer | Band 4 to 7 | Brings specialist skills in test strategy, automation, and exploratory testing. Helps the team build quality in from the start. Quality is a whole-team responsibility, not a separate gate. |

!!! info "Typical team shape"

    A DHCW product team typically includes 4 software engineers and 2 test engineers, plus the Product Owner and Delivery Manager. Larger services are made up of multiple such teams, not one large one.

## The attached capabilities

The following DDaT roles attach to a product team for the period their capability is needed. All of them are full members of the team for that period. They join the ceremonies, contribute to refinement, and take shared responsibility for the sprint goal.

| GDaD role | DHCW band | When they typically attach | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User Researcher | Band 6 to 8A | Heaviest in discovery and alpha, ongoing lighter presence in beta and live | Plans and runs research with users. Turns evidence into insights the team can act on. Ensures the team stays connected to the people using the service. |
| Interaction Designer | Band 6 to 8A | Discovery, alpha, and any beta feature with significant UI change | Designs how users interact with the service, both overall flow and individual elements. Works with Product Owners and Engineers to ensure interactions are well designed and achievable. |
| Service Designer | Band 7 to 8A | Discovery and alpha, and at points of significant service change in beta | Designs the end-to-end journey of a service across every touchpoint, channel, and team boundary. Maps how people experience a service in full, not only the digital parts. |
| Content Designer | Band 6 to 8A | Any phase where user-facing content is being created or changed | Makes sure the right information reaches the right people in the right way, in clear language, meeting Welsh Language Standards in both languages from the outset. |
| Business Analyst | Band 6 to 7 | Heaviest in discovery and alpha, present in beta for significant new features | Bridges product-level requirements and technical execution. Works with the whole team to develop user stories, acceptance criteria, and process flows. Story creation is a team activity, not a BA handoff. |
| Solution Architect | Band 7+ | Discovery and alpha to shape technical approach. Beta and live at points of significant design decision or where work touches national infrastructure or interoperability standards | Shapes the technical solution against the business need and the wider DHCW architecture. Ensures decisions are consistent with the DHCW Solution Architecture Strategy. |
| Technical Architect | Band 7+ | As above, with deeper focus on implementation-level design and technology choice | Works alongside the Senior Software Engineer to make technology choices that support long-term maintainability, portability, and open standards. |

At DHCW, architectural involvement is coordinated through the Technical Design and Assurance function. Teams should engage their allocated architect before committing to major technical direction that would be hard to reverse.

## When roles attach across the service lifecycle

The following table shows a typical shape of attachment across the service lifecycle. It is a starting point for planning, not a rule. Actual attachment is agreed with the Delivery Manager and the relevant Head of Profession.

| Role | Discovery | Alpha | Beta | Live |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product Owner | Core | Core | Core | Core |
| Delivery Manager | Core | Core | Core | Core |
| Software Engineer | Light | Core | Core | Core |
| Senior Software Engineer | Attached | Core | Core | Core |
| Test Engineer | Light | Core | Core | Core |
| User Researcher | Heavy | Heavy | Light | Light |
| Interaction Designer | Attached | Heavy | Attached (per feature) | Light |
| Service Designer | Heavy | Attached | Attached (at change points) | Light |
| Content Designer | Attached | Attached | Attached (per feature) | Light |
| Business Analyst | Heavy | Heavy | Attached (per feature) | Light |
| Solution Architect | Heavy | Attached | Attached (at change points) | Light |
| Technical Architect | Attached | Heavy | Attached (at change points) | Light |

Legend: Core means a permanent team member. Heavy means attached full time for this phase. Attached means attached as needed and joining ceremonies. Light means advisory only, attending key ceremonies.

## The Service Owner

Above the product team sits the Service Owner (typically Band 8B), who is accountable for how well the service meets user needs. The Service Owner is not a day-to-day team member but sets direction, represents the service to stakeholders and governance bodies, and holds accountability for outcomes. The Product Owner works within the direction set by the Service Owner.
